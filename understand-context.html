Day 1 · About 20 Minutes

Understand the Context

Source A · Presidential Statement

Lyndon B. Johnson

“Strength on the ground, strength in the air, strength at any time it may be needed. That is what we have working toward and for the purpose of convincing them (North Vietnam) of what we want.”

Students identify the message, intended purpose, and what this statement suggests about the president's approach to the war.

Open Library of Congress source ↗
Source B · Congressional Perspective

J. William Fulbright

“The country is gradually becoming quite disillusioned with it, as they should have been long ago.”

Students consider what Fulbright’s statement reveals about criticism and disillusionment within American political leadership.

CBS Face the Nation · April 27, 1969
Teacher Does

Present the Johnson and Fulbright excerpts side by side. Briefly identify each speaker and ask students to annotate language that reveals each speaker’s position, purpose, or attitude toward the war.

Students Do

Add both sources to the Vietnam War Evidence Chart. Compare what each source reveals, identify a key difference in perspective, and record evidence that supports the comparison.

Check for Understanding

Ask students to answer: How do these two sources complicate the idea that Americans shared one view of the war? Students must reference evidence from both excerpts.

Comparison Prompt

What does each source reveal about American political responses to the Vietnam War, and where do the two perspectives differ?
