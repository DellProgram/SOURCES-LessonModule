Part 1 · My Lai · About 25 Minutes

Compare the Accounts

Source A · Contemporary News Report

U.S. News & World Report, 1971

“That was my order, sir—that was the order of the day.”

The article reports on the My Lai prosecutions and includes Lt. William Calley’s courtroom explanation of his actions. Students examine what the report reveals about individual responsibility and how the event was being presented to the American public.

Source B · Legal Analysis

Kent A. Russell, 1970

“the military court alone ‘will not be competent’ to deal with this and other ‘vastly broader questions’”

Russell argues that My Lai raised questions beyond the guilt of individual soldiers and calls for a broader investigation. Students compare this interpretation with the contemporary reporting in Source A.

Teacher Does

Provide the selected My Lai excerpts and model one example of corroboration. Ask students to distinguish between what each source reports, what it argues, and what additional evidence would be needed before reaching a broader conclusion.

Students Do

Add both My Lai sources to the Vietnam War Evidence Chart and complete the corroboration comparison: one point of agreement, one difference or complication, each source's perspective, and one remaining question.

Check for Understanding

Each group identifies one claim the two sources can support together and one conclusion the evidence does not yet justify. Students must point to evidence from both sources.

Part 2 · Cambodia · About 15 Minutes

Connect Secrecy to Disillusionment

Students examine the supplied 1973 U.S. News & World Report article on the secret bombing of Cambodia. They identify evidence about secrecy, recordkeeping, the administration's stated rationale, and congressional criticism, then connect that evidence back to Fulbright's earlier statement about public disillusionment.
