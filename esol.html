ESOL Accommodations

Beginning ELs

More Visual + Language Support

  • Use the rally photograph and Women in Revolt poster as visual (SLIDE) support before moving into heavier reading and discussion tasks.
  • Preteach a small set of key words such as perspective, protest, evidence, corroborate, and limitation. Keep the words visible and pair them with a simple definition or visual example.
  • Shorten and chunk the LBJ, Fulbright, My Lai, and Cambodia excerpts. Highlight the lines students should use as evidence instead of expecting them to process a full dense passage at once.
  • Use leveled questions first: yes/no, either/or, pointing, or short-answer questions tied directly to the source. Build toward a short explanation after the student has identified the evidence.
  • Provide a word bank and sentence frames such as “This source shows ___ because ___” and “Source A is different from Source B because ___.” Allow one- or two-word responses before expanding them.
  • Give extra think time and let students rehearse with a partner before speaking to the class or writing independently.

Use: Know / Think / Wonder, visual analysis, the Evidence Chart, and guided source comparisons.

Intermediate ELs

Moderate the Language Demand

  • Keep students on the same historical sources, but break longer readings into shorter sections or bulleted chunks and rephrase directions when the wording is getting in the way of the history.
  • Keep academic vocabulary visible during the lesson and use the Evidence Chart as a graphic organizer students can refer to while reading, discussing, and writing.
  • Ask leveled questions that move from identifying evidence to explaining perspective and purpose. Give students additional think time before expecting a complete response.
  • Use sentence starters for academic discussion, such as “Both sources suggest ___, but ___” or “This source complicates the claim because ___.”
  • Use purposeful pairs or small groups for the My Lai and Cambodia comparisons so students can hear academic language modeled and rehearse their own response before independent writing.
  • Before the final response, allow students to make a brief bulleted summary of their evidence and then turn those notes into complete sentences.

Use: Understand the Context, Read Between the Lines, Corroborate and Refute, and the synthesis organizer.

Advanced ELs

Maintain the Grade-Level Task

  • Use the full class source set and the same historical thinking questions used with non-EL classmates.
  • Rephrase an unfamiliar term or question when needed rather than simplifying the historical thinking students are expected to do.
  • Use the Evidence Chart and synthesis organizer as planning tools, but expect students to independently explain perspective, corroborate sources, and qualify their claims.
  • During discussion, push students to use precise academic language such as perspective, corroborates, contradicts, limitation, and evidence.
  • Allow brief rehearsal before oral sharing when useful, then require an independent written response using multiple sources and perspectives.
  • Focus feedback on clarity and academic language while keeping the content expectations and final historical reasoning task the same.

Use: Corroborate and Refute, Establish a Plausible Narrative, and the final evidence-based response.

SLIDE / TREAD in This Lesson

When a task becomes TREAD-heavy—reading, explaining, asking/answering, or discussing—pair it with SLIDE support already built into the lesson: source images, highlighted text, visible vocabulary, modeling, and graphic organizers.

Additional Note

Use visuals and organizers universally when they help the whole class. Add sentence frames, word banks, modified text, or leveled questions as supplemental support based on proficiency. If a newcomer still cannot access the content after those supports utilize appropriate technology.
